1. Introduction & Platform Overview

1.1 Pedagogical Motivation & Supervisory Psychology

Modern education faces a profound challenge: while digital learning platforms provide unprecedented access to courseware, they simultaneously introduce cognitive fragmentation. Students regularly face digital distractions, screen fatigue, and micro-interruptions that undermine deep learning. In traditional classroom environments, teachers cannot continuously monitor thirty or forty students to determine who is cognitively immersed, who is taking active notes, who is quietly reflecting, and who is experiencing cognitive exhaustion or distraction.

CogniLearn AI bridges this gap by introducing a real-time, non-invasive cognitive attention tracking and telemetry platform. Unlike traditional punitive proctoring tools—which flag any averted glance as cheating or disciplinary violation—CogniLearn is built on Supervisory Ergonomics and Cognitive Psychology. The platform recognizes that human learning consists of diverse, healthy cognitive states:

  • Active Screen Focus: Gaze directed at instructional materials with alert blink rhythms.
  • Desk Work & Note-Taking: Downward head tilt with stabilized posture, indicating problem solving, calculation, or writing on paper.
  • Cognitive Reflection: Brief pauses (< 3.5 seconds) where the student glances aside or upward while synthesizing complex conceptual information.
  • Genuine Off-Task Disengagement: Sustained off-target gaze (> 3.5 seconds) or prolonged eyelid closure indicating fatigue or loss of focus.

1.2 System Objectives & Non-Punitive Philosophy

CogniLearn AI is explicitly designed as a formative self-regulation tool for students and a pedagogical awareness dashboard for educators. The primary engineering objectives are:

Student Core Formative Metacognition

Empowers learners to understand their cognitive rhythms, track daily deep work hours, identify peak hours of mental sharpness, and receive adaptive interventions (e.g., Box Breathing, 20-20-20 eye relief) when fatigue strikes.

Teacher Core Classroom Empathy

Provides teachers with live telemetry on collective class engagement, pinpointing lecture drop-off moments, identifying disengaged students who need extra support, and evaluating lesson comprehension in real time.

Ethical Design Guardrail
CogniLearn AI operates under strict ethical guidelines. Quantitative focus scores are never utilized for grade penalties or punitive actions. The system delivers actionable educational recommendations, positive gamified reinforcement (+XP), and formative feedback to nurture focus over time.

3.2 Perception to Telemetry Data Flow Pipeline

The lifecycle of every camera frame progresses through seven distinct stages:

  1. Video Frame Acquisition: MediaStream captured at 30 fps via `navigator.mediaDevices.getUserMedia` with mobile-compatible facing mode selection (`user` vs `environment`).
  2. Landmark Extraction: Frame passed to MediaPipe `FaceLandmarker.detectForVideo(video, timestamp)` executing WebGL GPU shaders to infer 468 mesh landmarks and 4x4 matrix.
  3. Metric & Posture Computation: Frame converted to isotropic pixel coordinates. 6-point EAR computed for ocular aperture; true Euler angles ($\text{Pitch}, \text{Yaw}, \text{Roll}$) extracted from rotation matrix.
  4. Cognitive Attention Inference: Metrics fed into the `CognitiveAttentionModel` sliding window (45 seconds). Dynamic EAR thresholding handles eyelid physiology; pitch variance distinguishes writing from head bobbing; Softmax calculates probabilities across 4 states.
  5. HUD Overlay Rendering: Canvas 2D context draws bounding brackets, eye mesh contours, iris center reticles, 3D directional gaze rays, and cognitive status pills.
  6. Telemetry Throttling: At a calibrated 1 Hz frequency, the active attention state is broadcast over Supabase Realtime websocket channels to educators.
  7. Diagnostic Session Logging: At session completion, aggregated telemetry timeline samples are committed to Supabase/MongoDB and processed by Gemini AI for report generation.

4. Deep-Dive: Classroom Tracking (Educator Experience)

4.1 Multi-Face Real-Time Detection & 6-Target Capacity

In classroom environments, an educator requires simultaneous oversight across the physical room. The ClassroomAutoTracker.tsx component interfaces with MediaPipe configured specifically for multi-face inference:

  • Configured via getMultiFaceLandmarker() with numFaces: 6, minFaceDetectionConfidence: 0.45, and minTrackingConfidence: 0.45.
  • Operates with dual GPU WebGL and CPU WASM fallbacks, ensuring resilience across educator laptops, school desktops, and tablets.
  • Processes all 6 student faces concurrently within a 33ms frame budget, extracting independent landmark sets, transformation matrices, and facial blendshapes.

4.2 DeepSORT-lite Spatial Tracking & Hungarian Bipartite Matching

A critical challenge in multi-student computer vision is identity switching—when two students move, tilt their heads, or cross paths, naive detectors interchange their names. CogniLearn implements a complete DeepSORT-lite tracking engine (in SpatialMultiFaceTracker within faceMatcher.ts):

[ Frame Detections ] [ Active Tracks ] Det 1, Det 2, Det 3 ... Track 1, Track 2 (Kalman Predicted) │ │ └─────────────────┬───────────────┘ ▼ [ Cost Matrix Calculation ] Cost = 0.50*(1 - IoU) + 0.20*(CentroidDist) + 0.30*(EmbeddingDist) * Spatial Gating: Zero IoU & CentroidDist > 1.5x Width -> Cost = 100,000 ▼ [ Hungarian Algorithm (O(N^3)) ] Solves optimal minimum-cost assignment ▼ ┌───────────────┴───────────────┐ ▼ ▼ [ Matched Pair ] [ Unmatched Detection ] • Update Kalman Filter • Spawn new TENTATIVE track • Append to Embedding Gallery• Requires >= 3 hits to become CONFIRMED • Apply Identity Hysteresis • Avoids phantom track creation

Identity Locking & Temporal Hysteresis:

To eliminate visual label flickering, each track maintains candidate matching counters. An identity is only assigned to a student once confirmed across consecutive frames. Once locked, the identity cannot be overridden by another student unless a candidate match exceeds the current match confidence by a significant margin sustained over 15 consecutive frames.

4.3 High-Tech Canvas AR Head-Up Display (HUD) Overlays

The classroom video stream is overlaid with a canvas rendering:

  • Target Bracket Reticles: Color-coded bracket corners around each student's face (Emerald for Screen Focus, Blue for Desk Work/Note-Taking, Amber for Cognitive Reflection, Coral for Off-Task).
  • Top Identity Pill: Displays student verification status, name, and match confidence percentage (e.g., ✔ Ananya Sharma (96%)).

4.4 DPDP Act 2023 Multi-Angle Biometric Calibration Wizard

To recognize students without storing invasive image libraries, CogniLearn features an on-device 5-sample biometric calibration flow:

Step 1–2 Frontal & Horizontal

Sample 1 captures primary frontal face geometry. Sample 2 requests a slight head yaw turn (left or right) to capture lateral facial proportions.

Step 3–5 Vertical, Expression & Confirm

Sample 3 requests a vertical pitch tilt; Sample 4 captures natural expression; Sample 5 synthesizes the final mean 512-D vector template.

All 5 samples are processed through the Umeyama 5-point alignment algorithm, cropped to 112x112, evaluated by MobileFaceNet into 512-D vectors, averaged into a single centroid vector, L2-normalized, and encrypted into IndexedDB.

6. Mathematical Formulations & Core Algorithms

6.1 6-Point Eye Aspect Ratio (EAR) & PERCLOS Drowsiness Metric

Eye openness is quantified using Soukupová and Cech's Eye Aspect Ratio (EAR) formulation, generalized across 6 landmarks per eye converted to isotropic pixel space:

Mathematical Formulation: 6-Point Eye Aspect Ratio $$EAR = \frac{\|p_2 - p_6\|_2 + \|p_3 - p_5\|_2}{2 \cdot \|p_1 - p_4\|_2}$$ $$\text{Left Eye: } p_1=33, p_2=160, p_3=158, p_4=133, p_5=153, p_6=144$$ $$\text{Right Eye: } p_1=263, p_2=385, p_3=386, p_4=362, p_5=373, p_6=374$$ $$avgEAR = \frac{EAR_{Left} + EAR_{Right}}{2}$$

PERCLOS (Percentage of Eye Closure): Drowsiness is not measured from individual blinks (which naturally last 150–300ms). Instead, PERCLOS aggregates the proportion of frames where $avgEAR < \text{Threshold}_{calibrated}$ across a 45-second sliding window buffer:

PERCLOS Temporal Aggregation Formula $$PERCLOS = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \mathbb{I}(avgEAR_i < \theta_{closed}) \quad \text{where } N \text{ spans } 45 \text{ seconds}$$ $$\text{If } PERCLOS > 0.35 \implies \text{Drowsiness Alert Triggered}$$

6.2 True Euler Angles (Pitch, Yaw, Roll) via 4x4 Matrix Decomposition

MediaPipe outputs a $4 \times 4$ column-major facial transformation matrix representing metric head rotation in 3D space. CogniLearn decomposes this rotation matrix $R$ into true metric Euler angles in degrees:

Euler Angle Matrix Decomposition $$R = \begin{bmatrix} d_0 & d_4 & d_8 \\ d_1 & d_5 & d_9 \\ d_2 & d_6 & d_{10} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} r_{00} & r_{01} & r_{02} \\ r_{10} & r_{11} & r_{12} \\ r_{20} & r_{21} & r_{22} \end{bmatrix}$$ $$\text{Pitch (X-axis)} = \text{atan2}(-r_{12}, \sqrt{r_{02}^2 + r_{22}^2}) \cdot \frac{180}{\pi}$$ $$\text{Yaw (Y-axis)} = \text{atan2}(r_{02}, r_{22}) \cdot \frac{180}{\pi}$$ $$\text{Roll (Z-axis)} = \text{atan2}(r_{10}, r_{11}) \cdot \frac{180}{\pi}$$

6.3 Umeyama 5-Point Similarity Alignment & Laplacian Blur Check

Prior to deep metric feature extraction, detected faces undergo optical gating and canonical alignment:

  • Laplacian Blur Gating: The nasal crop is convolved with a discrete $3 \times 3$ Laplacian kernel. The variance of the response measures edge sharpness: $$\sigma^2 = \frac{1}{M}\sum (\nabla^2 I - \mu)^2 \quad \text{where } \nabla^2 I = I_{x+1,y} + I_{x-1,y} + I_{x,y+1} + I_{x,y-1} - 4I_{x,y}$$ If $\sigma^2 < 32$, the crop is rejected for motion blur before model execution.
  • Umeyama Similarity Transform: Solves the least-squares orthogonal Procrustes problem to compute scale $s$, rotation $R$, and translation $\mathbf{t}$ mapping detected 5 landmarks (pupils, nose tip, mouth corners) to ArcFace $112 \times 112$ canonical reference coordinates.

6.4 MobileFaceNet 512-D Deep Metric Embedding & Cosine Similarity

Aligned $112 \times 112$ crops are normalized to $[-1, 1]$ floating-point tensors and evaluated by MobileFaceNet in ONNX Runtime Web. The resulting 512-dimensional output vector $\mathbf{v}$ is L2-normalized:

Deep Metric Biometric Similarity $$\hat{\mathbf{v}} = \frac{\mathbf{v}}{\|\mathbf{v}\|_2} = \frac{\mathbf{v}}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{512} v_i^2}}$$ $$\text{Cosine Similarity: } \text{Sim}(\hat{\mathbf{u}}, \hat{\mathbf{v}}) = \hat{\mathbf{u}} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{v}} = \sum_{i=1}^{512} u_i v_i$$ $$\text{Scaled Match Confidence: } \text{Confidence} = \max\left(0, \min\left(99, \left\lfloor \frac{\text{Sim} - 0.15}{0.65} \cdot 100 \right\rfloor\right)\right)$$

6.5 8-State Constant-Velocity Kalman Filter Formulation

Multi-target bounding boxes are tracked via an 8-state discrete Kalman filter implemented in BoundingBoxKalmanFilter:

  • State Vector: $\mathbf{x} = [c_x, c_y, w, h, v_{cx}, v_{cy}, v_w, v_h]^T$, where $(c_x, c_y)$ is box center, $(w, h)$ are dimensions, and $v$ are velocities.
  • State Transition: $\mathbf{F}$ advances center and size linearly: $c_{x, k} = c_{x, k-1} + v_{cx} \cdot \Delta t$ with $\Delta t = 1.0$ frame.
  • Innovation & Update: Measurement $\mathbf{z} = [c_x, c_y, w, h]^T$. Innovation residual $\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{z} - \mathbf{H}\mathbf{x}$ corrects the state estimate through Kalman gain $\mathbf{K} = \mathbf{P} \mathbf{H}^T \mathbf{S}^{-1}$.

6.6 Hungarian Algorithm (Kuhn-Munkres O(N³) Bipartite Matching)

The cost matrix $C$ associating $M$ detections with $N$ active tracks integrates spatial overlap, distance, and deep metric embedding similarity:

DeepSORT-lite Association Cost Function $$C_{d, t} = 0.50 \cdot (1 - \text{IoU}(B_d, B_t)) + 0.20 \cdot \min\left(1.5, \frac{\|\mathbf{c}_d - \mathbf{c}_t\|_2}{w_t}\right) + 0.30 \cdot (1 - \text{Sim}_{cos}(\mathbf{e}_d, \mathbf{e}_t))$$ $$\text{Spatial Gate: If } \text{IoU} = 0 \text{ and } \frac{\|\mathbf{c}_d - \mathbf{c}_t\|_2}{w_t} > 1.5 \implies C_{d, t} = 100,000 \text{ (Match Forbidded)}$$

The Hungarian algorithm solves minimum weight bipartite matching in $O(\max(M, N)^3)$ time using dual potential variables $(u_i, v_j)$, providing deterministic global optimum matching.

6.7 Cognitive Ergonomics Softmax Multi-Class Decision Tree

In cognitiveModel.ts, biometric features are evaluated across a multi-class probability tree:

  • Normalized probabilities: $P_{Screen}, P_{NoteTaking}, P_{Reflection}, P_{Distraction}$ computed from pitch angle, yaw, iris deviation, pitch variance, and temporal dwell seconds.
  • Softmax normalization ensures $\sum P_i = 1.0$. The winning state dictates the instantaneous target score.
  • Exponential Moving Average (EMA): Eliminates abrupt visual jumps between frames: $$\text{Score}_{EMA}(t) = \alpha \cdot \text{TargetScore}(t) + (1 - \alpha) \cdot \text{Score}_{EMA}(t-1) \quad (\alpha = 0.12)$$

7.2 Client-Side WebCrypto Encrypted IndexedDB Store

In compliance with the Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act 2023, student biometric embeddings are never transmitted to cloud servers. The client browser maintains a local encrypted IndexedDB database:

  • Database Name: cognilearn_biometrics_db (Object store: student_embeddings and crypto_keys).
  • Hardware-Bound Encryption: Managed via WebCrypto API (window.crypto.subtle) utilizing AES-GCM with 256-bit keys.
  • Payload Structure: Encrypted ciphertext array and random initialization vector (IV). Decryption produces only floating-point vectors: { studentId, meanEmbedding: float[512], exemplars: float[3][512], consentGiven: true }.
  • Zero Image Storage Guarantee: Raw webcam photos, video frames, or landmark images are never written to disk or storage.

8. Security, Privacy & Ethical Compliance Standards

8.1 Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act 2023 Adherence

Educational software handling biometric and behavioral telemetry must satisfy strict regulatory compliance. CogniLearn adheres to the Indian Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act 2023 through four technical pillars:

Pillar 1 Explicit Informed Consent

Biometric enrollment requires an explicit user consent checkbox. Consent status is cryptographically logged alongside the embedding record.

Pillar 2 Purpose Limitation

Perceptual telemetry is exclusively utilized to render live focus metrics and pedagogical reports. Data is never repurposed for advertising or commercial profiling.

Pillar 3 Right to Erasure ("Danger Zone")

Students retain complete self-service data erasure in AccountSettingsModal.tsx. Users type DELETE MY ACCOUNT to immediately purge all database records and IndexedDB keys.

Pillar 4 On-Device Data Localization

Biometric templates never leave the user's local device. Only anonymized, aggregated statistical numbers (e.g., avg focus 82%) reach the backend server.

8.2 LLM PII Pseudonymization & Langfuse Observability

When generating AI pedagogical reports in server/routes/report.ts:

  • PII Anonymization: All real student names, emails, and roll numbers are stripped and replaced with deterministic cryptographic hashes: crypto.createHash('sha256').update(studentId).digest('hex').substring(0, 10).
  • Deterministic Metric Truth: Average focus scores, duration minutes, drop-off times, and gaze shift counts are computed deterministically in TypeScript. The Gemini LLM is prohibited from computing or altering any numbers.
  • Zod Schema Enforcement: LLM outputs are validated against StudentNarrativeSchema and ClassroomNarrativeSchema. Any response deviating from required educational fields is rejected.

9. Project Guide Defense: Top 10 Technical Questions & Answers

This section prepares the student for project defense examinations, viva voce evaluations, and technical guide reviews with authoritative answers to the ten most challenging architectural questions:

Q 01 Why process computer vision in the browser rather than streaming video to a Python backend?
Answer: Processing computer vision client-side via MediaPipe WebGL and ONNX WebAssembly achieves three decisive advantages: 1) Latency: Frame processing occurs in under 33ms (real-time 30 fps) without network latency. 2) Bandwidth & Server Costs: Streaming high-definition video from 40 classroom students would require > 100 Mbps uplink and expensive server GPU clusters. Browser-native inference eliminates server video handling entirely. 3) Privacy by Design: Video frames never leave the user's device, ensuring zero video interception risks and compliance with DPDP Act 2023.
Q 02 How does the algorithm differentiate between a student writing notes on their desk vs playing on a smartphone?
Answer: The system employs a multi-feature ergonomic heuristic: 1) Pitch Angle: Note-taking typically exhibits downward pitch between $15^\circ$ and $35^\circ$ ($0.33 \le \text{effPitch} \le 0.54$), whereas looking at a smartphone concealed in one's lap produces steep downward drops ($> 45^\circ$, $\text{effPitch} > 0.56$). 2) Pitch Variance: Writing produces a stable, steady head posture with low pitch variance ($\sigma^2 < 0.005$). In contrast, phone browsing exhibits frequent upward glances (checking if the teacher is watching) causing high pitch variance. 3) Body Centering: Note-taking maintains centered lateral yaw ($\le 11^\circ$). Phone distraction often introduces off-axis head turns.
Q 03 How does the multi-student tracking prevent identity switches when students move or cross paths?
Answer: CogniLearn utilizes a complete DeepSORT-lite architecture in faceMatcher.ts: 1) Kalman Filter Prediction: Maintains an 8-state constant velocity motion model predicting where each student's bounding box will be in the next frame. 2) Composite Cost Matrix: Combines spatial overlap ($1 - \text{IoU}$), normalized centroid distance, and deep metric embedding similarity. 3) Hungarian Association: Solves global optimal matching via Kuhn-Munkres $O(N^3)$ bipartite assignment with spatial gating ($> 1.5\times$ face width forbids matching). 4) Identity Hysteresis: Track identities require continuous multi-frame confirmation to lock, preventing instantaneous ID flickering during occlusions.
Q 04 How do you prevent natural eyelid blinks from penalizing a student's focus score?
Answer: Natural human blinks last between 150ms and 300ms (spanning only 4–9 frames at 30 fps). CogniLearn prevents score drops via: 1) Temporal 45-Second Sliding Buffer: Instead of instantaneous thresholding, eye closure is evaluated via PERCLOS (percentage of closure aggregated over 45 seconds). A standard 200ms blink represents $< 0.7\%$ of this window, which is negligible. 2) Dynamic EAR Calibration: During the initial 25 seconds of a session, open-eye EAR values are sampled to calibrate individual eyelid baselines. 3) EMA Smoothing: The score updates via Exponential Moving Average with $\alpha = 0.12$, preventing single-frame drops from affecting metrics.
Q 05 What is the advantage of MobileFaceNet 512-D embeddings over simple geometric ratios?
Answer: While geometric landmark ratios provide a lightweight initial baseline, they are sensitive to facial expressions and illumination changes. MobileFaceNet is a convolutional deep metric neural network trained on millions of face pairs to map facial features into a 512-dimensional hypersphere where intra-person distance is minimized and inter-person distance is maximized. This provides robust invariance against lighting variations, smiles, and head tilts up to $\pm 30^\circ$.
Q 06 How does the teacher dashboard stream telemetry from multiple classrooms without server bottlenecks?
Answer: Rather than transmitting continuous video feeds or raw biometric matrices, the system transmits only lightweight 1 Hz throttled telemetry JSON packets: { studentId, state: "NOTE_TAKING", focusScore: 88, timestamp: 1728345600 } (approximately 90 bytes). These packets are broadcast via Supabase Realtime websocket channels. Even an entire school with 500 active students generates less than 50 KB/sec of aggregate websocket traffic, effortlessly handled on free-tier cloud infrastructure.
Q 07 How does the system handle students with different eye shapes and eyelid baselines?
Answer: Hardcoded EAR thresholds fail across diverse ethnicities. In cognitiveModel.ts, during the first 25 seconds of a session, CogniLearn samples all frames where the student faces forward ($\text{Yaw} < 22^\circ$) with open eyes. It computes the 10th percentile ($P_{10}$) of open EAR samples and sets: $$\theta_{closed} = \max(0.12, \min(0.22, P_{10} \times 0.85))$$ This dynamically calibrates an individualized threshold tailored to the student's unique ocular anatomy.
Q 08 Can the Google Gemini AI hallucinate student attention scores in diagnostic reports?
Answer: No. In server/routes/report.ts, all quantitative statistics (average focus score, peak focus, drop-off minute, gaze shift count) are calculated deterministically in TypeScript before the LLM is invoked. The system prompt strictly prohibits the model from computing or altering any numbers. Furthermore, the LLM output is validated at runtime using Zod schemas that reject any responses containing unauthorized quantitative fields.
Q 09 How is student data protected under the Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act 2023?
Answer: CogniLearn implements privacy by design: 1) Local Encryption: 512-D biometric vectors are encrypted using WebCrypto AES-GCM 256-bit keys and stored solely in local IndexedDB. 2) Zero Raw Photo Storage: Webcam photos and frames are never saved to disk. 3) PII Stripping: Student identifiers sent to the LLM are pseudonymized via SHA-256 hashes. 4) Self-Service Erasure: Students can permanently purge all account records and local keys via an interactive confirmation modal.
Q 10 How does CogniLearn support both secondary schools and universities?
Answer: In supabase/migrations/20261001_academic_cohorts.sql and src/config/academicCatalogs.ts, the data model supports hierarchical tier scoping: School Track: Configured with standards (Class 6–12), sections (A, B, C), boards (CBSE, ICSE, State), and foundational subjects (Physics, Mathematics, Social Science). College Track: Configured with degree programs (B.Tech, B.Sc, M.Tech), academic departments (Computer Science & Engineering, Electronics, Mechanical), semesters (1–8), and university subject catalogs. Cohorts are identified via unique 6-character alphanumeric codes (`SCH-XXXX` or `COL-XXXX`) allowing flexible institutional deployment.
